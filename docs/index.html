Before you arrive

Get ready in a few evenings

The camp assumes you're comfortable with basic Python. You need no machine-learning or robotics background — we teach all of that. If Python feels shaky, or you've never opened a terminal, spend a couple of hours on these first.

Do this one~2–3 hrs

Python basics

Get comfortable with variables, loops, functions, and lists. Pick one: read Automate the Boring Stuff (chapters 1–6), or do Kaggle's free browser course.

Automate the Boring Stuff ↗
Kaggle: Python ↗
Highly recommended~1 hr

What a neural network is

The best primer for Tuesday — all visual, no heavy math. Watch the first two episodes of 3Blue1Brown's Neural Networks series.

What you'll learn

From a single idea to a working robot

Each day builds on the one before. No step assumes anything you haven't done yet.

01

How machines learn

Neural networks, and how they train themselves from examples.

02

Computer vision

Train your own model to recognize the objects you photograph.

03

Edge AI

Shrink your model so it runs on a computer smaller than your hand.

04

Robotics

Connect what the model sees to what a robot arm does.

05

Your project

Build and present something that's yours.

By Friday you'll have: trained your own AI model, run it on an edge device, driven a robot with computer vision, and shown a working project.

How you'll work

Mostly, just your browser

Almost everything happens in your browser through our JupyterHub — Python, the GPU, and every lab are already set up there, so there's nothing to install. On the in-person days (Mon & Fri) you'll use the lab computers, so a personal laptop is optional — it's mainly so your setup carries over to the remote days (Tue–Thu) at home. (On Friday you'll also use SSH to reach the robot, set up together in person.)
